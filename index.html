Short bio

Haltya is Tommi Sirkiä's pioneering Finnish psy-funk project, running since 1996 and a founding pillar of the Suomisaundi sound. Early support from BBC Radio 1's Annie Nightingale launched a catalogue spanning Forest Flavour, Electric Help Elves, and Book of Nature, with live sets played across Europe, the Americas, Japan, and Australia. Latest: Space Travellers (2025, StereoHemia Records), a full joint album with fellow Helsinki veterans EvsY.

Space Travellers

StereoHemia Records December 2025 Psytrance 9 tracks

Not a remix comp, not a legacy repackage — a genuine collaborative record. Tommi Sirkiä (Justin Space) and Julius Lehmuskallio (Eraser) wrote and produced it together, with Jürgen Sachau (Frank Further) and Simo Ojala (Yöjalka) both back in the room on additional production, bringing the full creative history of both acts into one body of work for the first time. Recorded at Box Studio, mastered by Mickey Noise, cover art by Kensuke Takahashi.

Artist biographies

Haltya — est. 1996, Helsinki

Haltya

Built by Tommi Sirkiä and Jürgen Sachau, Haltya became one of the founding architects of Suomisaundi — the distinctly Finnish branch of psychedelic trance. Tommi (classically trained, backgrounds in rock, jazz, and music technology) and Jürgen (self-taught, instinct-driven) met in the middle: dense, groove-first psy-funk built on live guitars and horns laid into psychedelic production. Exogenic Records coined a term for it — "funky forest trance." Also called acid disco, acid funk, psy-funk.

Debut Project/Haltya 12" (1999/2000) picked up club support and BBC Radio 1 airplay. Forest Flavour (2000/01) and Electric Help Elves (2004, preceded by the single "Hero") followed. When Jürgen stepped back in 2005 to focus on Outolintu, Tommi carried Haltya forward as core producer — Book of Nature (2008) and Japan Anime Punk Sessions EP (2012) since. Three decades in, Haltya has played live and DJ sets in 18 countries, including Finland, Israel, Austria, Turkey, Russia, the USA, Japan, Brazil, Romania, Denmark, and Australia.

Side project — Highpersonic Whomen Tommi and Jürgen's club-facing progressive trance outlet, founded 2001. After Markku Louhio (Kiwa) joined, the sound pushed deeper and housey. Debut Alternative Energysource (2002) named a best-album-of-the-year by multiple trance critics; Push the Limit followed 2007.
Side project — Headphonics Tommi's breakbeat vehicle with Markku Louhio, formed after meeting in Brazil. "Kaikki tai ei mitään" (Headphonics RMX), ft. rapper Tommi H, hit #1 on Bassoradio's TOP-30 for six weeks — a rare jump from psytrance underground to Finnish national radio.
EvsY — est. 2000, Helsinki

EvsY (Eraser vs Yöjalka)

Julius Lehmuskallio and Simo Ojala, making psytrance since 2000 and still refusing to repeat themselves. Both classically trained, both drawn into the scene through Goa parties dating back to 1994, Eraser and Yöjalka built their sound on a shared conviction that psytrance should keep moving — pulling in heavy music, funk, and classical composition rather than settling into a formula.

Two albums and an EP on Helsinki's Exogenic Records, followed by a move to Australian label Faerie Dragon for Lords of the Liverdance (2006) and Virtuosi di Quosi (2009). EvsY's touring footprint runs Finland, Russia, Lithuania, Ukraine, Belgium, Israel, Turkey, Great Britain, Sweden, Denmark, Japan, and the USA — closely overlapping Haltya's own, no accident given two decades running parallel through the same Helsinki scene.
